Office K08

I am a Research Associate at the Institute of Astronomy at the University of Cambridge, applying cutting-edge statistical and machine learning techniques to analyse populations of type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia). My research sits at the interface of astrophysics and cosmology, focusing on understanding the physical properties of SNe Ia which allows us to use these events as accurate, precise cosmological probes.

BayeSN Development

My work predominantly focuses on the development and application of BayeSN, a next-generation, GPU-acclerated hierarchical Bayesian SED model for SNe Ia that disentangles the intrinsic properties of these events from the impact of astrophysical dust.

Preparing for LSST

Lead of the SN Ia Modelling Topical Team within the Dark Energy Science Collaboration (DESC), coordinating efforts to develop and validate supernova analysis tools for the Vera C. Rubin Observatory.

Research Interests

Effect of Environment: Investigating the link between properties of SNe Ia and their environments, improving our physical understanding

Lensed Supernovae: Resolving discrepancies between different methods of measuring cosmic expansion

Cosmological Constraints: Extracting astrophysically-robust cosmological measurements from large supernova datasets

Near-infrared SNe Ia: Analysing SNe Ia in NIR wavelengths, with improved colour information to probe intrinsic properties and reduced scatter

Broader Research

My PhD focused on population analysis of core-collapse supernovae discovered by the Dark Energy Survey, and I retain an interest in improved physical understanding of these events. My statisical expertise also enables collaborations beyond supernovae, including work focused on constraining galaxy morphology in the early universe using JWST data.
