Research

Simulation-Based Inference for Supernova Cosmology

Developing next-generation methods for cosmological parameter inference

Supernova Astrophysics and Cosmology

Current approaches to SN cosmology rely on multiple, disconnected steps. Simulations to characterise selection effects are based on a fixed fiducial model which is inferred separately to cosmological parameters. Astrophysical effects are already the largest source of systematic uncertainty in SN cosmology, and mistaken assumptions in the forward model arising from astrophysical unknowns could bias our results.

I am interested in simulation-based inference (SBI) as a way to account for astrophysical uncertainty and survey selection bias in supernova cosmology. In SBI, neural networks learn complex relationships between model parameters and observations from realistic simulations, allowing us to include effects that are difficult to express in an analytic likelihood.

I developed jax-SNANA to accelerate supernova simulations, light curve fitting and cosmological analysis with JAX. Its compiled simulator supports SBI workflows. My recent collaborative work includes FlowSN, which learns a likelihood that accounts for survey selection, and SBI modelling of SN Ia populations.

Selection Effects and Astrophysical Uncertainty

Magnitude limits mean that brighter supernovae are more likely to enter an observed sample. Realistic survey simulations allow us to account for this selection and for contamination from other types of supernova.

I am interested in including astrophysical effects in the same model used for cosmological inference. This allows uncertainty about the supernova population and host-galaxy dust to be propagated into constraints on cosmological parameters.
