Research

Supernova Host Environments

Understanding how SNe Ia are influenced by the environment in which they explode

Understanding the 'Mass Step'

After applying typical standardisation methods, we find that SNe Ia in massive galaxies appear systematically brighter (~0.05-0.1 mag) than those in low-mass galaxies. Understanding this effect is vital for both supernova astrophysics and cosmology; learning about how the properties of SNe Ia are affected by their environment will help inform us about their progenitor stars, while proper treatment of this dependence is vital to accurate distance estimation. Both intrinsic differences between SNe Ia and differences in dust extinction across host environments may contribute to this effect.

I have investigated this problem using BayeSN, a next-generation hierarchical Bayesian SED model for SNe Ia that separates dust extinction from the intrinsic properties of SNe Ia. We can apply BayeSN to large samples of SNe Ia to infer from data the relative contributions of these two effects.

Recent work extends this analysis to the ZTF SN Ia DR2 sample. In BayeSN × Dovekie, we jointly model photometric cross-calibration and the supernova SED, connecting model training to the calibration needed for precise distances.

Key Findings

Intrinsic SN Ia Differences

While dust may play a role, SNe Ia in different types of galaxy do seem to show intrinsic differences, pointing to some progenitor differences

Difference in i-band secondary maximum

Intrinsic differences are particularly strong (4.5σ difference) around i-band secondary maximum; understanding this may help explain what these progenitor differences are

Validation on Simulations

We apply BayeSN to simulations based on an alternative SED model, SALT, which incorporate dust and intrinsic differences, verifying that BayeSN is indeed able to distinguish these two effects separately

Impact

Cosmology: Accounting for intrinsic differences and dust extinction across host environments helps us estimate supernova distances more accurately. BayeSN is being integrated into DESC cosmological analysis pipelines.

Astrophysics: Evidence for distinct progenitor populations or environmental effects that affect explosion physics.
